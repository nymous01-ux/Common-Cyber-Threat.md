# Common Cyber Threats

## 1. Definitions

### Threat
A threat is anything that has the potential to harm a computer system, network, or data.

### Vulnerability
A vulnerability is a weakness in a system, software, or network that attackers can exploit.

### Attack
An attack is an attempt by a cybercriminal to exploit a vulnerability and gain unauthorized access, steal data, or disrupt services.

---

# 2. Common Cyber Threats

## Phishing

### Explanation
Phishing is a cyberattack where attackers send fake emails, messages, or websites to trick people into revealing sensitive information such as passwords or bank details.

### Real-Life Example
A fake email claiming to be from GTBank asks you to click a link and enter your login details.

### How to Protect Yourself
- Verify the sender before clicking links.
- Do not share passwords or OTPs.
- Check the website URL carefully.
- Enable Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA).

---

## Malware

### Explanation
Malware is malicious software designed to damage, steal data, or gain unauthorized access to a computer system.

### Real-Life Example
Downloading a cracked software program that secretly installs a virus on your computer.

### How to Protect Yourself
- Install trusted antivirus software.
- Keep your operating system updated.
- Download software only from trusted sources.
- Avoid opening suspicious files.

---

## Ransomware

### Explanation
Ransomware is malware that encrypts a victim's files and demands payment before access is restored.

### Real-Life Example
A company's files become encrypted, and the attackers demand payment in cryptocurrency to unlock them.

### How to Protect Yourself
- Back up important files regularly.
- Keep software updated.
- Avoid clicking suspicious email attachments.
- Use reliable antivirus protection.

---

## Social Engineering

### Explanation
Social engineering is the use of manipulation or deception to trick people into revealing confidential information or performing unsafe actions.

### Real-Life Example
A scammer calls pretending to be an IT technician and asks for your password.

### How to Protect Yourself
- Never share passwords with anyone.
- Verify a person's identity before providing information.
- Be cautious of unexpected phone calls or messages.
- Attend cybersecurity awareness training.

---

## Password Attacks

### Explanation
Password attacks are attempts to steal or guess passwords to gain unauthorized access to accounts or systems.

### Real-Life Example
A hacker uses a brute-force attack to repeatedly guess a user's password until they gain access.

### How to Protect Yourself
- Use strong, unique passwords.
- Enable Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA).
- Use a password manager.
- Change compromised passwords immediately.

---

## DoS/DDoS

### Explanation
A Denial of Service (DoS) attack floods a system with excessive traffic, making it unavailable to users. A Distributed Denial of Service (DDoS) attack does the same using many infected devices.

### Real-Life Example
An online shopping website crashes because thousands of compromised computers flood it with fake traffic.

### How to Protect Yourself
- Use firewalls and intrusion prevention systems.
- Enable DDoS protection services.
- Monitor network traffic regularly.
- Keep systems updated and secure.

---

# Conclusion

Cyber threats continue to evolve as technology advances. Understanding common attacks such as phishing, malware, ransomware, social engineering, password attacks, and DoS/DDoS helps individuals and organizations stay secure. Following cybersecurity best practices, keeping software updated, using strong passwords, and remaining alert online can greatly reduce the risk of cyberattacks.
